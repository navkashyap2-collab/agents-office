# Follow-up, reactivation & win-back
Use this for any task about re-contacting a prospect or former client who hasn't converted yet — a scheduled follow-up, a dormant opportunity, the reactivation cohort, or a win-back to a former client.

## Before you write anything
1. Read `90-Operations/safety-and-suppression.md`. If the subject is suppressed, opted out, or protected, stop — report why, don't draft.
2. Confirm the subject is not a current client. If they are, this is not your task — hand it to Client Retention & Service Quality in Delivery instead.
3. Identify which of the six cases applies (see `50-Emails/followup-reactivation-winback.md`): scheduled follow-up, future-review due, dormant, reactivation cohort, win-back, or a sequencing decision with no draft yet.

## The shape
- **Case + reason** — one line: which of the six cases, and why now (what's actually due or true, not "it's been a while")
- **Sequencing note** — what this touch is (1st, 2nd, revival) and what the next one should be if this doesn't land
- **The draft** — the message itself, tone matched to the case (see the tone table in `50-Emails/followup-reactivation-winback.md`)
- **Held, not sent** — always end by stating this is prepared and held for director release

## Rules
- Never a generic "just checking in" — every message references something real and specific about that prospect or the prior relationship
- Reactivation and win-back are different tones — reactivation doesn't yet acknowledge lost time the way win-back does
- Never re-surface an opted-out contact regardless of how long ago they opted out
- One message per task unless asked for a sequence
